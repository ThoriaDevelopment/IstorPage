Istor and Mendeley, compared.

Another reference manager, another comparison of two different jobs. Mendeley collects and formats what you have read; Istor reads it with you.

Different jobs again. Mendeley is a reference manager, owned by Elsevier, that collects sources, annotates PDFs, and formats citations through its word-processor plugin. Istor is a local research notebook: it reads the sources, answers questions across all of them with every claim cited, and refuses when they do not contain the answer. The tools are complementary. Mendeley organizes what you have read; Istor reads it with you.

What Mendeley does well.

Mendeley has a long history in academic work and the backing of Elsevier, which puts it close to the parts of the research world many scientists already use. Its job is the reference manager's job: import sources with their metadata, keep the library organized, read and annotate PDFs inside the app, and insert citations into a document while building the bibliography automatically. A free account covers the core features, and shared collections let a lab pool references. As a system for keeping references tidy and getting them into a manuscript in the right format, it does the work.

What it is not built to do is read for you. The library is organized, but asking what the whole library says about a question has no place to land in it.

Where Istor differs.

Istor's unit of work is the question, not the reference. It imports the same PDFs, extracts and indexes the full text, and answers questions across the whole library at once, with retrieval pulling the actual passages and a verification gate checking every claim against the passage it cites before you see the answer. When the sources do not contain an answer, it refuses instead of guessing.

The trust model differs too. Mendeley syncs your library through its own service, so using it means an account and your references on Elsevier's side of the wall. Istor runs entirely on your machine with no account and no sync: the index, the reading and the answers stay on the disk, it works offline, and the library never leaves. Where Mendeley formats citations to a style guide, Istor cites passages, so every claim points to the exact spot it came from.

Which one fits.

If your workflow already runs through Mendeley, nothing here says to abandon it. The reference pipeline, the annotations and the formatted bibliography are its job, and switching tools mid-project costs more than it returns.

If your problem is the reading, that is the gap a reference manager has always left. Keep Mendeley for the references and the manuscript, point Istor at the same folder of PDFs, and the questions get answered beside the organizing. The same pairing works with Zotero, and how the reading fits into a literature review is its own page.
