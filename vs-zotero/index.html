Istor and Zotero, compared.

This is the least adversarial comparison on the site. One tool organizes what you have read. The other reads it with you. Most serious setups want both.

Different jobs. Zotero is a free, open-source reference manager: it collects sources, keeps their metadata tidy, and formats citations and bibliographies in thousands of styles. Istor is a local research notebook: it reads the sources, answers questions over them with every claim cited, and refuses when they do not contain the answer. The tools are complementary rather than rivals. Zotero organizes what you have read; Istor reads it with you.

What Zotero does well.

Zotero has been the reference manager much of academia runs on for years, and the reasons are the same as ever. It is free, open source, and built by an independent nonprofit. A browser connector grabs a source's metadata in a click; collections, tags and notes keep a growing library navigable; and its word-processor plugins insert citations and build bibliographies in thousands of citation styles. Zotero 6 and later also read and annotate PDFs directly. As a system for collecting references and turning them into a correctly formatted bibliography, it is excellent.

What it is not built to do is read for you. Ask Zotero "what do my sources say about this" and there is nothing to ask, because managing references and understanding them are different problems.

Where Istor differs.

Istor's unit of work is the question, not the reference. It imports the same PDFs and papers, extracts and indexes the full text, and then answers questions across the whole library at once, with retrieval pulling the actual passages and a verification gate checking every claim against the passage it cites before you see the answer. When the library does not contain an answer, it refuses instead of guessing.

Where you work is different too. Zotero syncs through its own servers, which is convenient and requires an account. Istor runs entirely on your machine with no account and no sync, so it works offline and the library never leaves the disk. And where Zotero formats citations to a style guide, Istor cites passages: every claim points to the exact spot in the source it came from.

Which one fits.

If your problem is a bibliography, Zotero is the right tool and this page is not arguing otherwise. Collect with the browser connector, format with the plugins, and let it do the job it has done for years.

If your problem is that forty PDFs are sitting in a folder nobody has time to read, that is the job Istor takes. The two compose cleanly: keep Zotero for the references and the final formatting, point Istor at the same files, and the reading happens beside the organizing. How that looks in a literature review is its own page.
